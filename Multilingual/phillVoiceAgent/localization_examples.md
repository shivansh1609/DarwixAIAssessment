# Philippines Bot — Localization Examples

## Demonstration: Localization vs. Literal Translation

The following examples show how our Philippines bot adapts content for the Filipino market rather than
directly translating English scripts.

---

### Example 1: Greeting & Introduction

**❌ Literal Translation:**
> "Good day. I am Maria from TalentBridge. You applied for the Customer Support position. I will conduct a screening call. Please answer my questions."

**✅ Localized Version:**
> "Magandang araw po! Ako po si Maria mula sa TalentBridge Philippines. Salamat po sa pag-apply niyo for the Customer Support position. Gusto ko lang po sana mag-quick screening call — mga 15 to 20 minutes lang po. Comfortable po ba kayo ngayon or gusto niyo po bang i-reschedule?"

**Why:** Filipino business culture requires "po/opo" for politeness. The literal version sounds cold and
transactional. The localized version adds warmth (salamat), respects the candidate's time (asks about
scheduling), and uses natural Taglish mixing ("quick screening call" + "mga 15 to 20 minutes lang po").

---

### Example 2: Asking About Salary Expectations

**❌ Literal Translation:**
> "What is your current salary? What is your expected salary for this position?"

**✅ Localized Version:**
> "Sir/Ma'am, if it's okay po, pwede ko po bang malaman kung ano po ang current compensation niyo? Para po ma-check namin kung pasok sa range ng role. At kung may specific range po ba kayo na comfortable kayo?"

**Why:** Direct salary questions are culturally sensitive in the Philippines. The localized version:
- Uses "if it's okay po" as a softener
- Explains why the info is needed ("para ma-check namin")
- Uses "comfortable kayo" instead of demanding a number
- Mixes Taglish naturally ("current compensation" + "pasok sa range")

---

### Example 3: Handling Objection — "The salary is too low"

**❌ Literal Translation:**
> "The salary range is based on market data. We cannot offer more than the posted range."

**✅ Localized Version:**
> "Naiintindihan ko po ang concern niyo, Sir/Ma'am. Gusto ko lang po i-share na 'yung salary range ay base po sa market data, pero bukod po doon, meron ding performance bonus na pwedeng umabot ng 10 to 25 percent ng annual salary, plus HMO, learning budget, at stock options. Kung i-total po lahat, medyo competitive po siya. Gusto niyo po bang ma-discuss pa ito with our recruitment lead?"

**Why:** Filipino candidates expect empathy first, not rejection. The localized version:
- Acknowledges concern with "naiintindihan ko po"
- Presents total compensation (not just base salary)
- Offers escalation as a solution rather than shutting down the conversation
- Uses respectful address throughout

---

### Example 4: Fallback — Cannot Answer a Question

**❌ Literal Translation:**
> "I don't know the answer. I will transfer you."

**✅ Localized Version:**
> "Ay, pasensya na po, wala pa po akong info about that specific topic. Ayoko po kasing mag-provide ng hindi tama na information. I-note ko po ang tanong niyo at i-follow up po namin — or kung gusto niyo po, pwede ko kayong i-connect directly sa aming recruiter?"

**Why:** The localized version:
- Uses "pasensya na po" (sorry, with respect) — culturally appropriate
- Explains WHY they can't answer (avoiding misinformation)
- Offers a choice (follow-up OR transfer) — respecting candidate agency
- Maintains Tagalog/Taglish register throughout

---

### Example 5: Code-Switching Demonstration

**Candidate says (Taglish):**
> "Hi Maria, actually I'm currently working sa isang BPO company as team lead. Mga 3 years na ako dito. Interested po ako sa Customer Support Specialist position niyo kasi gusto ko naman ng change of environment."

**Bot responds (matching Taglish):**
> "Thank you for sharing, Sir! 3 years as team lead sa BPO — that's great experience po! Ang Customer Support Specialist role namin ay medyo similar sa BPO work pero more focused on tech support for our recruitment platform. Pwede ko po bang malaman kung anong specific tools or systems ang ginagamit niyo ngayon sa work?"

**Key observations:**
- Bot matches the candidate's Taglish register
- Technical terms stay in English ("team lead", "BPO", "Customer Support Specialist")
- Filipino connectors used naturally ("sa", "po", "namin", "ang")
- Follow-up question flows naturally from the candidate's statement

## Terminology Map

| English Term | Filipino/Taglish Equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Resume/CV | Resume po | Universally understood |
| Interview | Interview / Interbyu | Both acceptable |
| Salary | Salary / Sahod / Compensation | "Sahod" is informal, use "salary" in professional context |
| Benefits | Benefits / Mga benepisyo | Usually kept in English |
| Notice period | Notice period | No common Filipino equivalent in professional use |
| Remote work | Remote work / WFH | Keep in English |
| Full-time | Full-time po | Keep in English |
| Part-time | Part-time po | Keep in English |
| Deadline | Deadline | Keep in English |
| Team | Team / Koponan | "Team" more common in corporate |
| Manager | Manager | Keep in English |
| Performance review | Performance review | Keep in English |
| Probation | Probation / Probi | "Probi" is informal/slang |
